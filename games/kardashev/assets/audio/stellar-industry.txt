Zoom close, and the romance turns into logistics. Near Earth’s distance from the Sun, a complete sphere would cover about two point eight times ten to the twenty-third square meters. Capturing only one percent of the light still asks for roughly two point eight times ten to the twenty-first square meters of collector. Even impossibly light film averaging ten grams per square meter would mass almost three times ten to the nineteenth kilograms before adding trusses, cables, propulsion, factories, or radiators. That material cannot sensibly launch from Earth. Asteroids and perhaps whole small worlds become feedstock. Autonomous industry must mine, refine, print, inspect, dodge collisions, and repair itself for longer than any present institution has existed. And every habitat and computer must shed heat. Energy is plentiful; useful temperature differences, stable orbits, and trustworthy automation are precious. The iconic megastructure is only the visible leaf. The real organism is its supply chain.
