Our bridge to that future is already visible in miniature. In 2022, NASA’s DART spacecraft struck the asteroid moonlet Dimorphos and measurably changed its orbit: a first full-scale demonstration of asteroid deflection. The Deep Space Optical Communications experiment later returned data by laser from nearly five hundred million kilometers away, at rates far beyond comparable radio systems. On the Moon, today’s resource experiments are learning how local soil might provide oxygen, water, construction material, and propellant. None of these projects makes us Type I. They are rehearsal rooms. The true transition is when heroic demonstrations become ordinary services maintained through boring nights and bad budgets: supergrids, seasonal storage, carbon management, orbital traffic control, autonomous repair, and institutions trusted across generations. Only then can space stop being a chain of expeditions and become a durable industrial neighborhood. The first rung is a survival test disguised as an engineering project.
