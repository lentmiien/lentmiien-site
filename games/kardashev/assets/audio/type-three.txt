Now multiply by another ten billion. Type III, near ten to the thirty-sixth watts, is the equivalent output of roughly two point six billion Suns. The Milky Way contains somewhere around one hundred to four hundred billion stars and spans about one hundred thousand light-years, so this does not require engineering every system. It does require activity across a meaningful fraction of the galaxy. At that point there is no useful central control room. A message crossing the galactic disk needs one hundred thousand years at light speed. A reply doubles the wait. Local habitats, machine lineages, biological worlds, and archives must make their own choices while old instructions crawl through space. Type III is therefore not one colossal empire. It is a civilizational ecology: billions of stellar neighborhoods related by ancestry, protocols, stories, and occasional light. Its defining technology may be less a power plant than the ability to remain constructive while no one can ask headquarters what to do.
