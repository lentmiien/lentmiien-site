Then the scale detonates outward. Type II is not five hundred times Type I. It is ten billion times larger, near ten to the twenty-sixth watts. Our Sun radiates about three point eight two eight times ten to the twenty-sixth watts every second. Capturing a meaningful share suggests the most famous object in this story: a Dyson sphere. Forget the rigid shell from science fiction. A physically plausible version is a swarm—independent collectors, habitats, factories, computers, and radiators occupying many orbital families. It could begin with a few solar stations and grow for millennia. No single surface has to hold up against a star, and damaged units can be replaced locally. From inside, the solar system might look less like a machine than an archipelago of moving worlds, each with its own day, culture, and purpose. The star is not put in a box. Its light becomes the river feeding an entire stellar ecology.
