Look down at our planet after dark. Every bright road, furnace, data center, aircraft, farm, and warm apartment belongs to one unfinished machine. Averaged across a year, humanity now uses a little over twenty trillion watts of total power. Put that into the common continuous Kardashev formula and our coordinate is about zero point seven three. The last decimal is fuzzy because electricity, primary energy, and final energy count different things. The important view is the gap. Type I begins near ten quadrillion watts, roughly five hundred times our present throughput. That sounds colossal, yet the image already contains the seed: billions of people sharing grids, fuels, weather forecasts, supply chains, and one atmosphere. We are not outside the scale. We are a decimal civilization, powerful enough to alter a planet, but not yet coordinated enough to manage every consequence.
