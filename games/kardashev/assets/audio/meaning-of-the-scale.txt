Imagine this seed factory finishing its first antenna around another star. The message it sends home may arrive after everyone who launched it is gone. At one tenth the speed of light, simply crossing the Milky Way takes about a million years, before stopping to build anything. Expansion can spread in parallel, but values drift, machines fail, biospheres demand restraint, and descendants become strangers. This is where the meaning of the scale changes. Energy may become abundant while coordination, trust, and continuity remain scarce. Astronomers have searched infrared sky surveys for nearby galaxies whose starlight appears largely rerouted into warm waste heat. No obvious Type III system has emerged. That is evidence, not proof. Advanced societies might remain partial, cool, quiet, efficient, or simply unlike our guesses. Kardashev’s ladder is valuable precisely because it is incomplete: it tells us what large energy use could reveal, then forces us to ask what power alone cannot measure.
