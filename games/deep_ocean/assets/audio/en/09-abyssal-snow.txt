Away from the vent, the deep seafloor becomes a wide, patient plain. Food is scarce, but look closely: it is snowing. These flakes are not ice. Marine snow is a mixture of tiny organic pieces that began far above—plankton, waste, and other leftovers from the sunlit ocean. A flake may fall for weeks while microbes and small animals nibble at it on the way down. What finally reaches the bottom is precious. A sea cucumber gathers nutrition from soft mud and leaves a cleaned trail behind. A tripod fish can rest above the floor on three long fin rays, waiting for a meal to drift within reach. The dumbo octopus moves differently. Two ear-like fins paddle gently, while webbing between its arms helps it hover and steer. There is little reason to race when every movement spends energy. On this plain, patience is an adaptation. A falling crumb, a scent in the current, or a tiny movement in the sediment can be the beginning of dinner.
