At two hundred metres, the bright colors of the reef begin to disappear. Red fades first, then orange and yellow, until a dim blue is all that remains. Welcome to the twilight zone. There is light here, but not enough for photosynthesis, so no green meadow can grow. Instead, the water is filled with travelers. Lanternfish carry rows of light-making organs called photophores. From below, those tiny lights can match the faint glow overhead and erase the fish’s shadow. As evening reaches the surface, lanternfish, shrimp, jellies, and countless other animals begin swimming up to feed. Before sunrise, they descend again. Scientists call this diel vertical migration, and it is often described as the largest daily animal migration on Earth. Most of its travelers are small enough to fit in your hand, yet together they move a living layer through the ocean. Their commute also carries carbon from the bright surface into deeper water. Night after night, a hidden city rises, eats, and quietly returns.
