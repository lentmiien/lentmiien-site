Before we dive, stand where a river loosens into the sea. Rain that fell far inland has carried fresh water, leaves, and tiny nutrients to this sandy edge. The tide answers with salt water, so the mixture changes hour by hour. This is an estuary: part river, part sea, and one of the ocean’s gentlest front doors. Young fish shelter among eelgrass. Crabs search the wet sand. A sea star waits in a pool left behind by the tide. Beneath a ribbon of washed-up seaweed, speck-sized beach hoppers nibble and tidy. They are amphipods, small crustaceans with different kinds of legs. Keep them in your memory. Near the deepest floor of the ocean, their distant relatives will appear again. For now, take one last look at the sunrise. We are following the water outward, past the sandbars and waves, into the bright upper ocean. The surface is still close enough to glitter above us.
