Sunlight pours through the upper ocean and turns a coral reef into a busy neighborhood. It may look like a garden, but every coral polyp is an animal. One tiny polyp catches food with tentacles and builds a hard cup beneath itself. Thousands together can raise a reef that offers bedrooms, hiding places, and restaurants to other animals. Look between the tentacles of the sea anemone. A clownfish can shelter there because a special mucus coating helps protect it from stings. The partners help one another in several ways, a close relationship called symbiosis. Above them, a green sea turtle glides on long flippers. It lives in water, yet it is a reptile with lungs. Every breath waits at the surface. The sunlit zone reaches roughly two hundred metres down. Here, algae and microscopic phytoplankton use sunlight to make food. That energy travels through the whole neighborhood, from the smallest drifting life to fish, turtles, and animals much larger than our view.
