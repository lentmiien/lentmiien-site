The Moon looks close because it shares our sky, but its defining feature is the gap. On average, centre to centre, it is three hundred eighty-four thousand four hundred kilometres from Earth. Light crosses that space in about one point three seconds. Human voices sent through radio therefore return with a noticeable pause. Most diagrams pull Earth and Moon together; otherwise both become tiny on the page. Here, the darkness is the subject. Space is not the backdrop between objects. At cosmic scales, the interval often occupies almost everything.
