At half a millimetre, the invisible becomes a speck you can feel between your fingers—or in your shoe. Geologists define sand by size: from about zero point zero six two five to two millimetres, whatever mineral the grain is made from. This one is imagined as quartz, with worn edges and inclusions recording an untold journey through rock, river, and shore. Set beside a twenty-micrometre cell, it spans roughly twenty-five cell widths. Every beach is therefore a crowd of small landscapes, each grain far larger than the living rooms we just left.
