Now the scene becomes a living room—though nothing stays still. A typical animal cell is ten to thirty micrometres across. Inside, folded membranes build and move molecules. Mitochondria transform energy. Vesicles carry cargo. Fibres brace the cell and serve as tracks. The nucleus protects a working copy of the genome, but it is not a tiny manager issuing every command. Life emerges from many chemical conversations happening at once. Compared with a one-hundred-nanometre influenza particle, a twenty-micrometre cell spans about two hundred virus widths.
