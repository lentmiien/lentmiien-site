At twelve thousand seven hundred fifty-six kilometres across the equator, Earth finally fits into one frame. Mountains that filled a horizon barely texture its surface. The solid continental crust averages only about thirty kilometres thick—a fragile skin over mantle and core. Above it, the atmosphere has no visible political borders. Air and ocean move heat around the globe; weather links places that feel far apart. This view is powerful, but remember the trick: we had to leave every street, face, cell, and molecule behind to see the whole. A complete picture always trades detail for connection.
