At ten kilometres, no single person can take in every detail from the ground. A city becomes a pattern: blocks, bridges, parks, roofs, and rivers. Listen closer and the pattern is made of daily rituals—doors opening, kettles boiling, trains braking, shoes crossing pavement. Water pipes, power lines, food routes, and data links form an anatomy that no one sees at once. Unlike a whale, a city has no skin and no agreed edge. It grows through decisions. Scale becomes social: the distance between home and school, market and kitchen, neighbour and neighbour.
