At two nanometres wide, chemistry begins to feel like architecture. DNA’s two backbones twist into a double helix, while paired chemical bases make the rungs between them. The sequence of four base types stores instructions that cells can copy, repair, and read. Yet DNA is not a motionless ladder. It bends, vibrates, coils around proteins, and folds again so an enormously long molecule can work inside a microscopic cell. Information here is not floating abstraction. It has mass, shape, and a width—about two billionths of a metre.
