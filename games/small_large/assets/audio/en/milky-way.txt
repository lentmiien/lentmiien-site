At one hundred thousand light-years, stars stop being isolated points and become anatomy. The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy with dust lanes, star-forming regions, a central bulge, and a faint halo extending beyond the bright disk. Estimates place between one hundred and four hundred billion stars here. We cannot step outside to take its portrait. Instead, missions such as Gaia measure stars from within, using position and motion to infer the larger shape. Every unaided star in Earth’s night sky belongs to this one galaxy. The pale band overhead is our home seen edge-on, from inside the crowd.
