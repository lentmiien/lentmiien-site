Another one hundred thousand times outward brings us to the atom, around a tenth of a nanometre across. The nucleus holds almost all the mass, yet occupies only a minute fraction of the span. Electrons do not circle it on tidy tracks. Quantum mechanics gives us a probability cloud: regions where an electron may be found. That makes the atom spacious without making it empty of physics. Electric forces, wave behaviour, and exclusion rules give matter its shape. The chair beneath you feels solid because these diffuse atomic worlds resist being pushed through one another.
