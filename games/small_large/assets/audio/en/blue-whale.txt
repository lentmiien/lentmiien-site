Thirty metres is not just a building dimension. It can breathe. The blue whale is the largest animal known to live on Earth, and Antarctic individuals can reach around thirty-three metres. Yet this body is assembled cell by cell and nourished largely by krill only a few centimetres long. A feeding whale turns ocean water into a moving calculation: accelerate, open the pleated mouth, engulf tonnes of water, then strain the prey through baleen. Immense scale does not escape the small. It gathers the small, coordinates it, and makes a heartbeat that can travel through the sea.
