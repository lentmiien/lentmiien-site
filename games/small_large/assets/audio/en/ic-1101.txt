Our final outward stop is deliberately cautious. The giant elliptical galaxy I C eleven-oh-one has been described by NASA as spanning as much as four million light-years. But a galaxy is not a coin with a machined rim. Its light fades into a halo; stars mingle with the cluster around it; measurements depend on wavelength, distance, and the brightness threshold chosen as an edge. So “largest” is not a permanent trophy. The memorable idea is better: a galaxy can spread millions of light-years while refusing to tell us exactly where it ends. At the grandest scales, precision includes uncertainty.
