The Sun is about one point four million kilometres wide—roughly one hundred nine Earths across. What looks like a smooth disk from the ground is a shifting surface of hot plasma. Granules rise and sink. Magnetic fields twist. Prominences arc beyond the limb. Deep inside, fusion turns hydrogen into helium and releases energy that may wander for ages before escaping as light. Once free, that light needs only about eight minutes and twenty seconds to reach Earth. Our days, weather, food, and fossil fuels all borrow from the output of this ordinary, indispensable star.
