At roughly one hundred nanometres, an influenza virion is large compared with DNA and tiny beside a cell. Its genetic material travels in a protein package wrapped in membrane borrowed from a previous host. Surface proteins help it attach to another cell. Then the striking part: a virus does not carry the complete machinery to reproduce itself. It redirects the host’s molecular workshop. Viruses range from about the size of large protein assemblies to forms larger than some bacteria, so one number never describes them all. This is a scale waypoint, not a definition of life.
