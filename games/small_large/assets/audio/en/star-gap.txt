Now the numbers force a new unit. A light-year is distance: about nine point four six trillion kilometres. Proxima Centauri, the closest star beyond our Sun, is roughly four point two five light-years away. Light leaving there today will reach us more than four years from now. On a model where the Sun is a grain of sand, the next star would still be many kilometres away. Interstellar space is not perfectly empty; sparse gas, dust, magnetic fields, and cosmic rays pass through it. But compared with everything we have seen, the defining object here is absence measured across years of light.
