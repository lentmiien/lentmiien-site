# Research and source notes

Research was checked on 2026-07-29. The experience rounds distances and large
counts for young listeners, avoids unsupported claims about life, and
distinguishes measured facts from visual reconstruction.

## Narrative route

The story is an imagined guided tour, not a plotted spacecraft trajectory.
Alpha Centauri, TRAPPIST-1, Orion, and the Crab Nebula are selected
observational waypoints that teach how astronomers study star systems. After
those lessons, the narrative crosses the Milky Way’s disk conceptually and
enters its central regions. The opening narration, finale, media disclosure,
and README all state this choice.

## Claim map

### Milky Way and our address

- NASA describes the Milky Way as a spiral galaxy with a disk spanning more
  than 100,000 light-years and gives a Solar orbit of about 240 million years:
  [NASA Science · Galaxies](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/).
- NASA places the Sun in the Orion Spur between larger arms and describes the
  central bar:
  [NASA Science · The Milky Way Galaxy](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/the-milky-way-galaxy/).
- The tour uses the repository-consistent rounded value of about 230 million
  years from NASA’s current Solar System facts:
  [NASA Science · Solar System facts](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/).
- No outside photograph of the Milky Way exists. Every face-on image is a model
  or artist reconstruction because observers live inside the disk.

### Gaia

- ESA reports more than three trillion observations of two billion stars and
  other objects from 2014-07-27 to 2025-01-15. ESA also estimates that two
  billion stars are about one percent of the Milky Way’s stellar population:
  [ESA · Gaia factsheet](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia/Gaia_factsheet).
- The exact number and prominence of spiral arms remain active research. The
  story therefore says the map is improving rather than presenting one arm
  diagram as final.

### Alpha Centauri and Proxima b

- NASA gives Proxima Centauri as the closest known star beyond the Sun, a
  little over four light-years away, and describes Proxima b’s roughly
  eleven-day orbit and habitable-zone location:
  [NASA Exoplanets · Proxima b](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/eso-discovers-earth-size-planet-in-habitable-zone-of-nearest-star/).
- “Habitable zone” is carefully explained as a temperature-distance clue, not
  proof of water, air, or life. Proxima b’s surface in the generated scene is
  explicitly unknown and imagined.

### TRAPPIST-1 and planet detection

- NASA’s current Webb explainer describes seven Earth-size, probably rocky
  planets, all orbiting closer to their star than Mercury does to the Sun, with
  periods from about 1.5 to 19 days:
  [NASA Webb · TRAPPIST-1](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/science-overview/science-explainers/what-is-webb-revealing-about-the-trappist-1-system/).
- NASA explains that a transit is a repeated dip when a planet crosses its
  star and that the dip helps reveal orbital period and planet size:
  [NASA Exoplanets · What is a transit?](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/whats-a-transit/).
- The artwork contains exactly seven planets after a reviewed correction. No
  ocean, atmosphere, or biosphere is claimed.

### Orion Nebula

- NASA identifies the Trapezium’s four massive young stars, the sculpting
  effect of ultraviolet light, and the role of protoplanetary disks:
  [NASA Hubble · Orion Nebula](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/hubbles-sharpest-view-of-the-orion-nebula/).
- NASA reports that Hubble has resolved proplyds around nearly 200 Orion stars:
  [NASA Hubble · Planetary construction zones](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-highlights/finding-planetary-construction-zones/).
- The generated view enlarges proplyds and combines wavelength aesthetics so
  children can read the concept; it is not a single exposure.

### Crab pulsar

- NASA gives a distance of about 6,500 light-years and a pulsar rate of 30
  pulses per second:
  [NASA Science · Crab Nebula](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/crab-nebula/).
- The narration distinguishes the energized pulsar torus from a black-hole
  shadow because their visual rings could otherwise confuse young viewers.
- The image is a multiwavelength artist interpretation based on X-ray and
  optical structure, not a literal naked-eye view.

### Central Molecular Zone

- ESO’s 2026 ALMA release maps molecular gas in the extreme and chaotic
  Central Molecular Zone and assigns different display colors to different
  molecules:
  [ESO · Largest ALMA image of the CMZ](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso2603a/).
- ESO’s VISTA notes describe dense stars, branching dust, the Arches Cluster,
  and roughly one thousand hot young stars:
  [ESO · Galactic Center detail](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso0949f/).
- The generated colors translate infrared and radio information. The scene
  explicitly states that human eyes would see a darker view.

### Sagittarius A*

- ESO’s GRAVITY observations give a black-hole mass of 4.30 million Suns and a
  distance of 27,000 light-years:
  [ESO · Stars orbiting Sagittarius A*](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2119/).
- ESO reports that S2 completes an orbit in about 16 years and that its path
  tests general relativity near the black hole.
- The EHT/ESO release describes eight linked radio observatories used for the
  2017 observations, the 2022 image release, a four-million-Sun mass, and the
  dark shadow surrounded by glowing radio structure:
  [ESO and EHT · First image of Sagittarius A*](https://www.hq.eso.org/public/news/eso2208-eht-mw/).
- The generated climax combines an EHT-like asymmetric ring, an infrared-style
  star field, and a representative orbit trail. It is disclosed as a composite
  artist interpretation rather than the published EHT image.

## Language and child-safety choices

- The text uses “center” in the display title exactly as requested.
- Large numbers are spoken slowly and rounded.
- Black holes are described as regions of extreme gravity, not monsters that
  indiscriminately “suck up” the galaxy.
- Wrong quiz choices receive “A new discovery!” and the same complete
  explanation as correct choices. No score or failure language is shown.
- “Habitable” never means “inhabited,” and life claims are avoided.
