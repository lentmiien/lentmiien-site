Now let the view pull back.

The glowing center, the dusty spiral arms, the Orion nursery, the Crab’s flashing core, the seven TRAPPIST-1 worlds, Alpha Centauri, and our small Solar System all belong to one Milky Way.

We did not make a physical flight. The distances are far beyond any journey people can take today. Instead, we traveled with light.

A tiny dip in starlight uncovered a planet. A repeating pulse revealed a spinning neutron star. Infrared and radio wavelengths opened curtains of dust. The motion of one bright star weighed a black hole that could not be seen. When many telescopes and many people combined their measurements, the hidden galaxy became a little clearer.

Our pictures were artist interpretations, but the clues beneath them are real. And the map is not finished. We still debate the exact shape of our spiral arms. We still study whether distant rocky worlds have atmospheres. We still ask how stars can form so near a supermassive black hole.

That is the best part of reaching the center: it gives us new questions to carry home.

Whenever you are ready, begin again—or look up tonight and remember that you are already inside the adventure.
