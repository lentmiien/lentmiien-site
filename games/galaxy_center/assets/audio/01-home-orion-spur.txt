Ready? Our journey begins without leaving home.

Look at the Milky Way spread across the darkness. This view is an artist’s map, built from many telescope measurements. No spacecraft has ever flown far enough to turn around and photograph our whole galaxy.

The Milky Way is a vast family of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter. Its bright disk stretches more than one hundred thousand light-years across. Our Sun lives in a smaller lane called the Orion Spur, about twenty-six thousand light-years from the crowded center.

That sounds far away because it is. Even light, the fastest messenger we know, needs about twenty-six thousand years to travel from the galactic center to Earth. The light reaching us tonight began its trip long before people built cities.

We will not pretend our imaginary ship can cross that distance for real. Instead, we will travel the way astronomers do: by following light, measuring motion, and testing clues.

Home signal steady. Curiosity switched on. Let us begin by visiting the nearest star family beyond our Sun.
