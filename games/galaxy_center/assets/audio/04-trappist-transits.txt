Forty-one light-years from Earth, a tiny red star keeps one of the best-studied planet families beyond our own.

Its name is TRAPPIST-1. Seven roughly Earth-size, rocky worlds circle it. The entire family is astonishingly compact: all seven planets orbit closer to their star than Mercury orbits our Sun. Their years last from about one and a half days to roughly nineteen days.

How did astronomers find worlds that are lost in their star’s glare? They watched the light. When a planet crosses in front of TRAPPIST-1 from our viewpoint, it blocks a tiny fraction of the star. The same little dip repeats each orbit. Its timing reveals the planet’s year, and its depth helps reveal the planet’s size.

Space telescopes have measured the planets’ masses, sizes, temperatures, and hints about their atmospheres. Yet the biggest questions remain open. We do not know whether any has an ocean, comfortable air, or life.

So notice the difference between knowledge and imagination. Seven planets are real. Their sizes and paths are measured. The detailed surfaces in our artwork are possibilities, not discoveries.
