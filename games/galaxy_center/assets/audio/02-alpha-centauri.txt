Our first stop is the Alpha Centauri system, the nearest known star family to our own.

Alpha Centauri A and B are two bright, Sun-like stars held in a long gravitational dance. Farther from that pair travels a much smaller, cooler red dwarf: Proxima Centauri. At about four point two four light-years away, Proxima is the closest known star beyond the Sun.

Proxima has at least one fascinating world. Proxima b is probably rocky, and its year lasts only about eleven Earth days. It orbits in the star’s habitable zone, where temperatures might allow liquid water under the right conditions.

But “habitable zone” does not mean “second Earth.” Proxima often produces powerful flares, and we do not yet know whether Proxima b has an atmosphere, an ocean, or anything alive. The planet in this scene is therefore an artist’s careful guess, not a photograph.

Already, one nearby system has surprised us: three very different stars, moving together, with a world circling the faintest one. Before we travel farther, let us unpack the enormous ruler we just used.
